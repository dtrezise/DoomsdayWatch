# Research methodology and verification standards

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-01

## Question and scope

Do independently measurable departures from normal preparedness indicate a specific approaching threat beyond publicly documented risks? Use public, lawful sources. Assess geography, time horizon and mechanism separately. The current sample is purposive and cannot estimate global threat prevalence.

## Evidence and judgments

`confirmed_fact`: narrowly supported by an inspected primary record; confirms what the record establishes, not every institutional assertion. `reported_claim`: attributed assertion whose underlying occurrence has not been independently established. `analytical_inference`: reasoned interpretation with premises and alternatives. `speculation`: hypothesis lacking sufficient supporting evidence; excluded from key judgments.

Verification states: `unverified`, `partially_verified`, `verified`, `contradicted`, `withdrawn`. Verification applies to an atomic proposition. Partial verification never licenses the unsupported part. Confidence is `not_assessed`, `low`, `moderate`, or `high` and refers to evidentiary support, not probability of catastrophe. High confidence requires direct, specific evidence and resolved material conflicts; moderate means credible but incomplete evidence; low means weak provenance, ambiguity or substantial gaps. A well-documented official statement can be confirmed while the statement's underlying allegation remains reported.

## Collection and review

1. Extract one testable proposition per claim; preserve research lead ID. Separate award value, obligations, deliveries and operational activation.
2. Locate original records; use authoritative reporting for context. Inspect full text and exact dates. Search snippets are discovery evidence only and cannot verify occurrence.
3. Register URL, publisher, publication date, access date, relevant section, access outcome and independence group. Keep short excerpts or paraphrases within copyright limits. Opaque nonportable citation IDs are unusable.
4. For consequential claims seek a primary record and independent reporting. Identify common upstream origins; two outlets repeating one statement are one evidence chain.
5. Record corroboration, contradiction, alternative explanations and explicit disconfirmation tests. Failed retrieval is not disproof. Check mirrored documents and archives before declaring a source unavailable.
6. Capture dated snapshots and SHA-256 hashes for newly saved evidence. Record original URL and capture time; never imply a snapshot exists when it does not. This initial release registers URLs and retrieval notes, not a complete evidence archive.
7. Check the cutoff. Quarantine future-dated, inconsistent, dynamic or missing content. A live portal is not a historical all-clear.
8. Compare matched 2023–2025 windows and units before anomaly classification. Document denominator, coverage, seasonal effects, inflation, award cycles and policy changes. Avoid selecting only alarming sectors or dates.
9. Review claims and update correction entries. Keep old conclusions in version history; materially changed judgments need an explanation.

## Anomaly assessment

Assess evidence reliability, baseline magnitude, urgency, independent corroboration and threat specificity separately. Use qualitative values with rationale; leave unmeasured dimensions `not_assessed`. Do not average them into a threat probability. A candidate stays `not_assessable` until a comparable baseline and observed value exist. Distinguish announcements from actual activity; use initiating grid events separately from orders and extensions. Evaluate dependent signals as a cluster rather than multiplying them.

## Competing hypotheses

H1: Routine resilience policy, exercises and procurement cycles explain clustering. H2: Public conflicts and known infrastructure risks explain specific changes. H3: A new shared threat explains otherwise unexplained operational deviations. H4: Selection effects or erroneous unverified claims create the pattern. Seek evidence against each; H3 requires timing, mechanism and independent operational corroboration. Preparedness language alone has low threat specificity.

## Baseline design

For each series preserve unit, geography, source coverage, calendar/fiscal year, event definition and revisions. Compare January–October 2026 with January–October 2023–2025 where possible; full-year historical milestones provide context only. Three years are a short, conflict-affected reference period, not a stationary normal. Never compute statistical significance from a few selected announcements. Missing data constrain conclusions.

## Reporting and monitoring

Reports state cutoff, scope, key judgments, confidence, evidence references, alternatives, gaps and change triggers. Every factual assertion links to claim/source IDs and exact URLs. Editorial review precedes external distribution. The scheduled investigative task may publish bounded documentary updates after reviewing source support and passing checks; ambiguous or consequential new interpretations remain in the research queue for review. Monitoring should fetch allowlisted sources, preserve raw responses and hashes, validate timestamps, deduplicate origin chains, flag changes for review and never automatically promote a claim or infer imminent war. Retrieval errors generate data-quality flags, not threat alerts. Keep quiet when unchanged; notify only on meaningful verified changes or required action once monitoring is enabled.
