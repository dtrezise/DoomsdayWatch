# DOE’s grid emergency order register shows a rise in paperwork

November 9, 2026 · Research record

The Department of Energy's Section 202(c) registers list 65 order documents issued in January–October 2026. The same window contains 1 document in 2023, 1 in 2024 and 11 in 2025. The comparison measures federal order paperwork. Extensions and repeat orders can concern the same facilities or reliability problems.

| January–October | Register entries | Listed order documents |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 1 | 1 |
| 2024 | 1 | 1 |
| 2025 | 9 | 11 |
| 2026 | 62 | 65 |

The [row-level ledger](../evidence/doe_202c_register_jan_oct_2023_2026.csv) records issue dates, the order identifiers named in each entry, and the number of documents per entry. The 2023 and 2024 registers each describe one order without printing its number in the register entry. The two Puerto Rico documents issued November 6, 2026 fall outside the matched window. We counted documents with letter suffixes as separately issued documents and flagged them in the ledger. A new number can also continue an earlier intervention. For example, the February, May and August 2026 Campbell entries repeatedly address keeping the same plant available.

The earlier 1/1/9/14 series cannot be reproduced as a consistent January–October document count. Nine is the 2025 register-entry count. The claimed CRS table remains unavailable. The DOE register comparison supports a rise in administrative use of this authority. Determining whether separate grid emergencies or actual dispatch increased requires reviewing signed orders, linking renewals, and joining dispatch and reliability records. The orders themselves give specific reasons including weather, anticipated load and plant availability.
